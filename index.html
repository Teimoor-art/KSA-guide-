Where to go

Six faces of the Kingdom

Saudi Arabia is not one landscape but many — glass towers, holy cities, rose-red canyons, and a coastline built for diving. Here's where each region earns its place on the map.

01
Capital Region

Riyadh

A skyline that didn't exist a generation ago — King Abdullah Financial District, Boulevard City, and a nightlife scene built almost entirely in the last decade.

02
Red Sea Coast

Jeddah

The Kingdom's cultural port city — Al-Balad's coral-stone old town, corniche sunsets, and the gateway most pilgrims pass through.

03
Holy Cities

Makkah & Madinah

The spiritual center of the Islamic world, and the reason most first-time visitors come at all — access is reserved for Muslims.

04
Heritage Site

AlUla

Carved tombs older than Petra, a valley of sandstone monoliths, and the Kingdom's biggest bet on tourism-led reinvention.

05
Eastern Province

Dammam & Khobar

The oil coast — Gulf waterfronts, causeway access to Bahrain, and the Kingdom's most international corporate culture.

06
Southern Highlands

Abha & Asir

Fog-covered mountains, terraced villages, and a cooler climate that surprises anyone who thinks Saudi Arabia is only desert.

Getting in

Visa & travel, without the guesswork

Most nationalities can now apply for a Saudi e-Visa online, arriving on a single-entry or multiple-entry tourist visa valid for a year. Requirements differ for work, Umrah, and family visit visas — here's the shape of it.

  • Tourist e-Visa: online application, usually approved within minutes to a few days
  • Umrah visa: tied to an approved operator or the Nusuk platform
  • Work visa: sponsored by an employer, processed through the Ministry of Human Resources
  • Passport validity: at least six months recommended at time of entry
Living it right

Customs worth knowing before you land

Saudi Arabia has opened up quickly, but a few customs still shape daily life and are easy to get right once you know them.

Dress

Modest clothing is expected in public for everyone. Abayas are no longer mandatory for women, but shoulders and knees covered is the safe default.

Prayer times

Shops and restaurants may pause briefly five times a day. It's brief, predictable, and rarely disrupts a normal day out.

Weekend

The work week runs Sunday to Thursday, with Friday and Saturday as the weekend — plan flights and meetings accordingly.

Hospitality

Arabic coffee and dates are a genuine welcome ritual, not a formality — accepting a small cup with the right hand is polite, not obligatory.

Working here

Vision 2030 and the job market it built

Saudi Arabia's Vision 2030 program has poured investment into tourism, entertainment, tech, and giga-projects like NEOM and Qiddiya — reshaping what "working in Saudi" even means.

  • Sectors hiring internationally: construction, healthcare, tech, hospitality, education
  • Employer-sponsored work visas (Iqama) are the standard route
  • Salaries are typically tax-free on income, a major draw for expats
  • Saudization quotas mean some roles prioritize local hires first
The pilgrimage

Planning for Hajj & Umrah

Hajj happens on fixed Islamic-calendar dates and is obligatory for eligible Muslims; Umrah can be performed any time of year and takes far less planning.

  • Hajj: booked through official government quota systems and approved operators
  • Umrah: apply via the Nusuk platform or a licensed travel agent
  • Peak Umrah season is Ramadan — expect larger crowds and higher prices
  • Package pricing varies widely by proximity to the Haram and group size
