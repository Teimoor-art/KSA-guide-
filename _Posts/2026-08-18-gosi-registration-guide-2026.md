GOSI, the General Organization for Social Insurance, is Saudi Arabia's social insurance system, and understanding how it applies to your employment helps clarify what's being deducted from your salary and what it covers.

## What GOSI Covers

GOSI provides coverage for:

- Occupational hazards (workplace injury and disability coverage)
- Pension and retirement benefits (primarily applicable to Saudi nationals)
- Unemployment insurance (SANED), which applies to both Saudi and non-Saudi employees in the private sector

## How Registration Works

Employers are responsible for registering employees with GOSI as part of onboarding, typically completed alongside other employment paperwork like the labor contract and Iqama processing. Employees generally don't need to register independently — it's handled through the employer's GOSI account.

## Contribution Rates

Contribution percentages differ between Saudi nationals and expatriate employees, reflecting the different benefits each group is entitled to. Saudi employees contribute toward both pension and unemployment insurance, while expatriate contributions are generally more limited, focused mainly on occupational hazard coverage, with rates set and periodically adjusted by GOSI regulations.

## Checking Your GOSI Records

Employees can check their registration status and contribution history through:

1. The GOSI website or mobile app
2. Logging in with your Iqama number or national ID and registered credentials
3. Viewing your employment and contribution history under your profile

## Why This Matters for Expats

Even though expatriate employees generally aren't eligible for the pension portion, GOSI registration still matters because it confirms your employer has properly registered your employment — an important record if you ever need to file a labor dispute or verify your work history.

## SANED Unemployment Benefits

The SANED program, integrated with GOSI, provides temporary financial support to eligible employees who lose their jobs, calculated based on prior salary and contribution history. Eligibility rules and payout periods are set by GOSI and are worth reviewing directly if you're facing potential job loss.

## A Practical Tip

Periodically check your GOSI record through the app to confirm your employer's contributions are up to date — discrepancies are easier to resolve early than after months have passed, especially if you're planning to change jobs or file any related claims.
