Driving in Saudi Arabia gives expats a level of independence that public transport in most cities still can't fully match. Here's how the licensing process works in 2026.

## Two Ways to Get Licensed

Expats in Saudi Arabia generally have two paths to legally drive:

### 1. License Exchange
If you hold a valid driving license from certain countries with a reciprocal agreement, you may be able to exchange it directly for a Saudi license without taking a test. This is the fastest route and typically requires:

- Your original foreign driving license and passport
- A valid Iqama
- An eye test at an approved center
- A translated copy of your license (if not in Arabic or English)

### 2. Taking Driving Lessons and a Test
If your home country isn't on the exchange list, you'll need to enroll in an accredited driving school, complete a set number of lessons, and pass both a theory and practical exam.

## Where to Apply

Applications are processed through the Saudi Traffic Department (Muroor), and appointments can often be booked via Absher. Some steps may also be available through licensed driving institutes that handle documentation on your behalf.

## Documents You'll Typically Need

- Valid Iqama
- Passport copy
- Passport-sized photos
- Blood type certificate (required in some cases)
- Eye examination result

## Cost and Timeline

Costs vary depending on whether you're exchanging a license or starting from scratch with lessons. An exchange is usually completed within a single visit, while the full course-and-test route can take a few weeks depending on scheduling.

## Tips for a Smooth Process

- Double-check whether your nationality qualifies for direct license exchange before booking driving lessons — this alone can save weeks
- Bring official translations of any documents not in Arabic or English
- Book your eye test and Muroor appointment through Absher in advance to avoid walk-in queues

## Driving Culture Note

Saudi roads use the right-hand side, similar to the US and most of Europe. Speed limits and traffic laws are strictly enforced with automated cameras, so it's worth familiarizing yourself with local road signs before your first drive.
