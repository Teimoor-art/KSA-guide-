Jeddah has always had a different personality from Riyadh — more coastal, more historic, and long known as the Kingdom's cultural gateway. Here's what to prioritize on a visit.

## Al-Balad — Jeddah's Historic Heart

Al-Balad, the city's old town, is a UNESCO World Heritage site known for its coral-stone buildings and intricately carved wooden balconies (roshan). Wandering its narrow alleys in the evening, when the area comes alive with cafes and small galleries, is one of the best ways to experience old Jeddah.

## The Jeddah Corniche

Stretching for kilometers along the Red Sea, the Corniche is where locals go to walk, cycle, and watch the sunset. It's also home to public art installations and open-air spaces that make for an easy, relaxed evening out.

## King Fahd Fountain

One of the tallest fountains in the world, the King Fahd Fountain shoots seawater over 300 meters into the air and is best viewed from the Corniche in the evening when it's lit up.

## Red Sea Diving and Snorkeling

Jeddah is one of the best access points to the Red Sea's coral reefs, which are considered among the healthiest in the world. Several dive centers offer trips for both beginners and experienced divers, with visibility that rivals more famous diving destinations.

## Jeddah Waterfront and Floating Mosque

The Al Rahma Mosque, often called the Floating Mosque, sits right at the edge of the Red Sea and is a popular photo stop, especially around sunset when the water reflects the building's white architecture.

## Tahlia Street and Modern Jeddah

For shopping, dining, and a taste of the city's contemporary side, Tahlia Street is lined with cafes, restaurants, and boutiques — a good contrast to the historic feel of Al-Balad.

## Practical Tips for Visiting Jeddah

- The best months to visit are between November and March, when the coastal heat and humidity ease off
- Al-Balad is best explored on foot in the early evening, both for the light and the cooler temperatures
- Jeddah is also the main entry point for pilgrims heading to Makkah, so airports and roads can be busier during Hajj and Ramadan periods

## Final Thought

Jeddah rewards visitors who slow down — pairing a walk through Al-Balad's centuries-old alleys with a sunset by the Corniche captures the city's blend of history and coastal life in a single day.
